ARGO

A hundred eyes on Rome’s metro.

ARGO reads live CCTV and flags violence, weapons and snatching within seconds, so the operator acts on the moment instead of reviewing it afterwards.

I

The neighbourhood

Termini, the Metro B platforms, the escalators at rush hour: the same scenes repeat every day. A phone pulled from a hand as the doors close, a bag lifted in the crowd, an argument that turns into a fight on the mezzanine. The State has answered with more police in the stations, and since January 2025 the area around Termini is a reinforced-surveillance zone. Crime has not gone away. The weak point is not the number of cameras, it is the attention behind them: one operator cannot watch hundreds of feeds, and most footage is reviewed only after the fact, when the damage is done. ARGO starts from there.

      III

      Weapons

      Statistics count crimes, not the tools used to commit them. The Ministry of the Interior sorts Rome’s crimes into 34 categories, and none of them separates offences committed with a weapon: nobody can say how many robberies on the metro involved a knife. A camera can see it, if someone is watching. That is why ARGO treats weapons as a separate signal: a dedicated detector looks for pistols, knives and rifles in every frame, and a weapon only counts when it is in someone’s hand.

        IV

        How it works

        ARGO does not watch pixels the way a person does. It turns every frame into skeletons, reads 1.6 seconds of movement at a time, and asks two networks and a weapon detector the same question: is someone in danger right now?

          VI

          Who would use it

          ARGO is built for law enforcement and for whoever sits in front of the camera wall: metro and station control rooms, local police, private security. Instead of searching dozens of feeds, the operator receives the event: which camera, what is happening, how confident the system is. The same alert can be pushed by software to officers in the field, so the patrol closest to the platform knows before anyone calls. ARGO does not decide and does not intervene: every alarm is checked by a person.

          Limits and next steps

          Fights are ARGO’s strength: 90% accuracy on 30-second clips it has never seen. Armed violence and snatching are still weak (F1 0.10 and 0.13) because public footage of them is scarce, and the WEAPON WARNING catches only a small share of real weapons. On a laptop the panel runs with a delay. Next: real footage from a transport operator, a zoom on the hands for small weapons, a larger detector on GPU, and alerts delivered straight to officers’ devices.

          Measured during development

          On a frame of Largo Argentina filmed from above, the YOLO26s detector finds 22 people and the YOLO26s-pose model 0: with small, distant people the pipeline can lose them.

          Tools and artificial intelligence

          Trained on Kaggle’s free GPUs, on public datasets only. Built with Claude Code as programming assistant: every design choice was made, measured and logged by the author, and every number on this page comes from a test the code can repeat.

          • VisionUltralytics YOLO26s-pose, fine-tuned YOLO26s (weapons), TrackTrack tracker
          • ModelsPyTorch: ST-GCN (chosen) and Graph-Transformer; torchvision R(2+1)D-18 (Kinetics-400) fine-tuned on violence, on the pixels
          • ComputeKaggle, 2 T4 GPUs
          • MapsOpenStreetMap (ODbL)
